GIFCT Definitional Frameworks

A research tool for counter-terrorism teams worldwide, making global legal definitions of terrorism and violent extremism quick to access, compare, and digest, without embedding bias into the interface.

The Outcome

Launched at the GIFCT's annual global summit, the tool became the most widely adopted digital release in the organisation's history.

The Challenge

The Global Internet Forum to Counter Terrorism (GIFCT) is an NGO founded by Facebook, Microsoft, Twitter and YouTube in 2017 to prevent terrorists and violent extremists from exploiting digital platforms.

They had developed a definitional framework of global terrorism and violent extremism legislation. The brief was to translate that research into tangible online tools, ready in time for the GIFCT's annual global summit, where it would debut to thousands of member organisations.

The interface had to support governments, researchers, NGOs, and trust-and-safety professionals with very different mental models, without embedding bias into how sensitive data was presented.

"Support my efforts and speed me up by easing complexity."
Tech company employee, discovery interview

The Solution

An interface that supports multiple data views and element-level filtering, so users can shape output to their workflow while the underlying semantic framing stays neutral and transparent.

  • Map, chart, and table views of the same core data
  • Element-level filters for precise legislative comparisons
  • Distinct palettes for terrorism vs. violent extremism data
  • Connections out to partner research (e.g. GNET) to avoid dead ends
  • Bespoke Umbraco build the GIFCT team can update without slowing down

Discovery

Two parallel tracks: interviews to understand the work, and desk research to expand the problem space beyond it.

User interviews

Research interviews with counter-terror professionals from member organisations including Airbnb and Twitter. The aim was to:

  • Surface user requirements and opportunities to exceed the brief
  • Get immersed in industry language, mental models, and processes
  • Understand the pain points specific to counter-terror work
  • Prioritise ideas against genuine user need

Desk research

After interviews, I used desk research to diverge, following up terminology and patterns surfaced in conversation, and looking at how others had solved adjacent problems. This helped:

  • Map where the wider industry's frustrations sit now and where they're heading
  • Expand on the strongest interview findings before ideation
  • Study how other domains handle layered disclosure of sensitive data

User personas

I'm sceptical that personas belong in every project, but here they helped the creative team and the client share a clear view of who we were solving for, and what each group actually needed from the data.

Persona 01

Tech company employees

"Support my efforts and speed me up, by easing complexity."

Need flexible, fast-to-navigate data and clearly defined resources they can browse without missing context.

Persona 02

Researchers & expert practitioners

"Tell me more, lead the way. Be an authoritative voice."

Need data that's easy to read and compare to spot emerging trends, with the source's neutrality and credibility made explicit.

Persona 03

Governments & stakeholders

"Show me the value of our work."

Need to understand the data at a glance and feel confident their work is being made accessible to partner companies and working groups.

Definition & structure

A prioritised brief, a sitemap, and content modelling, delivered to the client alongside the discovery findings so they could follow the reasoning behind every decision.

"I can" user stories

Functionality was prioritised against user-led "I can" statements. A useful frame when working with complex data visualisation.

Sitemap & content model

The sitemap communicated the underlying decision model, and content modelling defined templates and components for the fullest viable build inside the deadline.

Objectives

Maximise the value drawn from the underlying research, integrate with users' existing workflows, and connect out to partner insights so users never hit a dead end.

Testing, iteration & handover

A first prototype and a first developed iteration were tested with stakeholders and users. Their feedback drove pre-launch changes, including a second colour palette to clearly differentiate violent extremism data from terrorism data.

Handover included a measurement plan covering events and metrics for Google Analytics, Tag Manager, and HotJar, with each metric tied to a "most likely outcome" criterion. That gave the GIFCT team the tools to direct iteration after launch with confidence.
